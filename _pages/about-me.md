I am a Ph.D. candidate in Chemical and Biomolecular Engineering at **Johns Hopkins University**, advised by Prof. Konstantinos Konstantopoulos, and an experimentalist with 7+ years of research in quantitative imaging, hydrogels, polymer systems and cell mechanobiology.

Research
------
My doctoral work investigates how microenvironmental cues (**viscosity, confinement, stiffness and electric fields**) regulate cancer cell migration.

* **Fluid viscosity and invasion.** I showed that elevated extracellular fluid viscosity enhances 3D cancer cell invasion through KCC4-dependent signaling, and mapped ion channels (NHE1, KCC4, SWELL1) and cytoskeletal regulators by immunofluorescence and live-cell imaging.
* **Matrix stiffness.** I formulate tunable polyacrylamide hydrogels that match healthy and tumor-associated breast tissue stiffness, and characterize them by rheology to link matrix mechanics to cell behavior.
* **Electric fields.** I designed a microfluidic electrotaxis device to study how electric fields affect confined cancer cell migration, contractility and calcium signaling.
* **Optogenetics.** I engineered optogenetic constructs for targeted protein activation, recruitment and degradation, quantified by live-cell confocal imaging.
* **From dish to organism.** I standardized workflows across 2D culture, microfluidic migration assays, 3D collagen-embedded spheroids and zebrafish xenografts, with quantitative QC metrics to benchmark reproducibility.

This work has also generated data for one funded NIH R01 and three additional NIH R01 applications.

Background
------
Before Johns Hopkins, I earned my B.S. and M.S. in Chemical Engineering at **IIT Kanpur** with Prof. Rahul Mangal, where I studied active Janus colloids and self-propelled liquid-crystal droplets in complex fluids using rheology, optical microscopy and SEM. I then worked as a Post-graduate Engineer at **Sterlite Technologies** on optical fiber manufacturing, and as a Project Associate at IIT Kanpur.
