Research at the interface of engineering, cell biology, and the physical sciences.

Bishwa Ranjan Si

Doctoral Researcher, Johns Hopkins University

I investigate how cancer cells integrate mechanical, ionic, electrical, and metabolic signals to migrate through complex tissue environments.

Biography

I am a doctoral researcher in the Department of Chemical and Biomolecular Engineering at Johns Hopkins University. I use engineering approaches to study how tumor microenvironmental cues regulate cancer-cell migration, invasion, and resilience.

My work combines microfluidic confinement, quantitative live-cell imaging, molecular perturbations, metabolic assays, three-dimensional culture systems, and zebrafish and chick-embryo models. My broader goal is to identify physical and metabolic adaptations that enable cancer dissemination and reveal new therapeutic vulnerabilities.
