I study how cancer cells sense and adapt to the physical properties of their microenvironment. My research combines mechanobiology, cell and molecular biology, microfluidics, advanced imaging, metabolic analysis, and in vivo models to understand how mechanical cues reshape cell migration, invasion, and resilience.

About me

I am a doctoral researcher in Chemical and Biomolecular Engineering at Johns Hopkins University in the Konstantopoulos Laboratory. My work examines how dynamic tumor microenvironments regulate cancer-cell migration, with emphasis on extracellular fluid viscosity, confined migration, ion transport, cell-volume regulation, mechanical memory, and metabolic rewiring.

Before moving into cancer mechanobiology, I studied active colloids and self-propelled droplets. That background continues to shape my interest in connecting active matter, cell motility, and disease progression.
